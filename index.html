How We Evaluated Increasing Actuator Production from 380 to 450 Units per Shift

We investigated what it would take to produce 70 more actuators per shift, without overlooking testing capacity, staffing limits, or Quality requirements.

Our team studied the assembly process, reconstructed functional-test workload, reviewed historical Quality records, and evaluated seven possible line configurations. We used those findings to make a decision grounded in the evidence, not just the production target.

PROJECT ABSTRACT

What we set out to improve, how we studied it, and what we found

Mixed-model actuator line7 feasibility milestonesCross-functional decision process

Our goal was to evaluate a production increase from a 380-unit operating reference to 450 Quality-accepted actuators per regular shift. We needed to determine whether the whole assembly and testing system could support the target, not simply whether one station could work faster.

We mapped the six-step production flow, reconstructed the time each actuator occupied the functional-test fixture, checked staffing and equipment alternatives, and reconciled historical test records. The analysis exposed a gap between programmed test time and real fixture demand. Because no complete, qualified configuration demonstrated the target, we recommended specific evidence gates and a management-held strategy rather than launching an unsupported upgrade.

01 / Project brief

The problem: a 70-unit gap in the production plan

We evaluated a proposed increase from the conditional 380-unit operating reference to 450 unique Quality-accepted actuators per regular shift. The assessment required evidence of a complete, staffed and qualified production configuration, not an isolated station calculation.

Our evaluation criteria

We needed a complete production configuration that aligned fixture capacity, labor availability, valid evidence and Quality acceptance.

We treated production capacity as a system feasibility problem. A single-station improvement could not validate the output target if shared resources, staffing or downstream acceptance remained constrained.

02 / Investigate

We traced the assembly process to find the real bottlenecks

We traced the six operation groups, variation in qualified staffing, the handling of test failures and the difference between programmed test time and total fixture occupation. This established the real questions our capacity model needed to answer.

SYSTEM CONSTRAINT

Functional test

Test programming was only part of the actual fixture workload. Loading, connection, unloading, repeats, diagnostics and intercycle activity demanded separate evidence and resource accounting.

Field insight / 01

Staffing flexibility depended on qualifications and work overlap.

We evaluated changes in operator assignments, variant mix, support duties and shared-resource timing. The model had to account for work conflicts that station averages could hide.

Field insight / 02

Programmed test time understated total fixture occupation.

We separated program execution from connection, loading, unloading, intercycle time, diagnostics and repeat tests to reconstruct a more useful workload reference.

03 / Trust the data

We verified test records before using them as evidence

We reconciled the serial-level test population before using the data in a capacity argument. We kept repeat tests, missing histories, inspections and Quality acceptance separate so the analysis would not overstate valid output.

Evidence integrity
Test passes and Quality-accepted units are different measures.

The controlled Phase 2 population contained 1,137 unique tested serials. Of those, 123 histories were incomplete and required two different limitation classes. Those gaps stayed visible all the way through closeout.

Cross-functional leadership

We aligned engineering decisions with functional authority.

Quality controlled acceptance and evidence release. Operations controlled the daily executable. Maintenance retained fixture corrective action. Finance retained receivables and cost reconciliation. Document Control preserved released baselines.

Commercial judgment

We screened commercial requests against scope and resource limits.

We separated controlled information requests from technical execution and warranty commitments. When proposed terms introduced unlimited audit, retention or capacity obligations, we maintained the approval boundaries and closed the negotiation without award.

08 / The outcome

What we delivered and what remained unresolved

We completed the feasibility scope and delivered a documented management decision.

The integrated assessment did not demonstrate a complete configuration capable of sustainably producing 450 unique Quality-accepted actuators per regular shift. The feasibility work was completed and formally closed. Option C remained a strategic watch, Stage V closed without execution, and every continuing obligation had an accepted owner.

The recorded operating history totaled 109,760 unique Quality acceptances across 289 regular shifts, with 192 consecutive fault-free FT-A01 shifts. These operating observations did not close the permanent corrective action or certify sustainable 380- or 450-unit capability.

09 / Methodology

What guided our engineering decisions

Our working approach followed three principles: investigate the full production system, make every assumption and evidence limitation traceable, and package technical conclusions into decisions with explicit owners.

01.

Understand the whole system.

We mapped the process sequence and treated fixture occupation, qualified staffing, upstream work and Quality disposition as connected capacity constraints.

02.

Make evidence traceable.

We classified recorded facts, reconstructed estimates, uncertainty bounds and hypothetical scenarios separately, preserving incomplete histories and Quality definitions.

03.

Make decisions executable.

We translated analysis into reviewed alternatives, staged approval gates, resource ceilings, ownership transfers and a management recommendation supported by the evidence.

How we structured the work

We organized the investigation around shop-floor process mapping, traceable test-record populations, reconstructed equipment workload and integrated configuration reviews. Our final recommendation separated observed operating performance from conditional capacity estimates, and assigned each unresolved responsibility to its functional owner.
